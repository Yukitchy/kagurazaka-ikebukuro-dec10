Temple · Bishamonten

ZENKOKU-JI

善國寺

The temple that watches over Kagurazaka. Founded in 1595, it enshrines Bishamonten, counted among the Shinjuku Yamanote Seven Lucky Gods. Look at the gate: where most temples keep guardian dogs, this one keeps stone tigers.

Gates 7:00 to 19:00

Gates 7:00 to 19:00; the hall is open 9:00 to 17:00 (temple listing on Enjoy Tokyo, checked on 11 Nov 2026)

02
Shrine · old and new

AKAGI SHRINE

赤城神社

A shrine founded in 1300, one minute from Kagurazaka Station — and a small surprise for anyone who likes buildings: its hall was rebuilt in 2010 with glass walls, in a project supervised by architect Kengo Kuma. Seven centuries of shrine, in very modern dress.

Grounds open 24 hours

The grounds are open at all hours; the shrine office is open 9:00 to 17:00 (shrine listings, checked on 11 Nov 2026)

03
Stone-paved back lanes

THE LANES OF KAGURAZAKA

兵庫横丁・かくれんぼ横丁

Step off the main slope and Kagurazaka turns into lanes: Hyogo Yokocho in the photo, and Kakurenbo Yokocho — “hide-and-seek lane” — nearby. Stone paving, black wooden fences, traditional restaurants behind them: what is left of the old geisha district. Hyogo Yokocho takes its name from the armoury that stood here in the days of Ushigome Castle.

Open streets · walk any time

Where we eat — we pick together

Lunch is not booked and not decided — that is the plan, and there are two places it can happen. Four of the picks are in Kagurazaka, close to the morning walk: hand-made soba, set meals in a renovated old house, buckwheat galettes in Tokyo’s French corner, and iekei ramen up the slope. The other four are in Ikebukuro, where the afternoon happens anyway: an old-school tonkatsu counter, the famous ramen queue, a top-100 yoshoku hamburg, and conveyor-belt sushi a short walk from Sunshine City. We look, we talk, we choose — by appetite, and by queue. Tap a tile for details.

Soba · hand-made

Kyorakutei

石舅掃き手打 蔵楽亭

Hand-made soba, milled in the shop, in a small room of 27 seats — the soba name Kagurazaka locals give first. The ten-zaru, soba with tempura, is the order.

Lunch 11:30 to 15:00, Tuesday to Saturday · closed Sundays, Mondays and holidays
Kagurazaka 3-6, a few minutes from the lanes

¥¥ Lunch ¥1,000–¥2,000

Cash only, and small — we aim a little before 12:00.

Set meals · matcha sweets

Kagurazaka Saryo

神楽坂 茶尔 本店

A renovated old house serving Kyoto-style obanzai set meals — rice with a spread of small seasonal dishes — and the matcha sweets it is known for. The calm, sit-down Japanese option.

11:30 to 22:00, every day (food last order 20:00)
Kagurazaka 3-1, just off the main slope

No reservations — walk-in only, so we go a little before 12:00.

Galettes · from Brittany

Le Bretagne

ル・ブルターニュ 神楽坂店

Buckwheat galettes and sweet crêpes, served the Breton way in Kagurazaka, Tokyo’s French corner. The galette complète — ham, egg and cheese folded into a crisp square — is the classic, and there is Breton cider for the grown-ups.

Weekdays 11:30 to 22:00 (last order 21:30) · open every day
Kagurazaka 4-2, on the main slope

¥¥ Lunch ¥1,000–¥2,000

Reservations accepted — and sweet crêpes for dessert.

Ramen · Yokohama iekei style

Montana

神楽坂家系ラーメン モンタナ

Kagurazaka’s iekei ramen, in a room that looks more like a bar than a noodle shop. The broth is the Yokohama style — rich tonkotsu-shoyu with thick noodles — and you call the details yourself: noodle firmness, soup strength, oil. The Montana-men is the full works — chashu, nori, spinach and a seasoned egg.

Lunch 11:00 to 16:00, every day · no fixed closing day
Kagurazaka 5-8, up the main slope — 4 min from Iidabashi Station

¥¥ Lunch ¥1,000–¥2,000

No bookings — ticket machine at the door. Closing days are irregular, so we check this one before setting out.

Tonkatsu · Ikebukuro classic

Shimizuya

清水屋

A plain, old-school tonkatsu shop up on the second floor, four minutes from Ikebukuro Station — and the one its regulars swear by. Tabelog readers rate it the best tonkatsu in Ikebukuro, and the plates explain it: thick, crisp cutlets, the rosu-katsu set meal under ¥1,000 with ¥100 off at lunch, and a katsu curry that half the room is eating. Twenty-five seats, no frills.

Monday to Friday 11:00 to 20:00 (last order 19:30) · closed Sundays — and closes when the day’s stock runs out
Higashi-Ikebukuro 1-8-7, 2F — 4 min from Ikebukuro Station East Exit

¥¥ Lunch ¥1,000–¥2,000

No bookings — and cash only: no cards, no e-money.

Ramen · Ikebukuro’s famous queue

Mutekiya

無敵家

Ikebukuro’s famous ramen queue, five minutes from the East Exit. Mutekiya has served rich tonkotsu-shoyu bowls since 1998 — counter seats only, seventeen of them — and the staff take your order while you line up, so the bowl arrives soon after you sit. To be straight about it: at lunch, 15 to 25 minutes in line is normal. We pick this one only if the queue looks kind and everyone is up for it.

10:30 to 03:30, every day
Minami-Ikebukuro 1-17-1 — 5 min from Ikebukuro Station East Exit

¥ Lunch around ¥1,000

No bookings — the queue is the booking. Cash only.

Yoshoku · Ikebukuro hamburg

UCHOUTEN

ウチョウテン

A small yoshoku room south of the station, on Tabelog’s list of Japan’s top 100 yoshoku restaurants, and built around one plate: a kuroge wagyu hamburg steak in demi-glace, cut open and running with juices. Western-style cooking the old Japanese way — careful, unhurried, and worth the short walk.

Lunch 11:30 to 14:30 (last order 14:00), Monday to Saturday · closed Sundays
Minami-Ikebukuro 2-36-10 — 7 to 8 min from Ikebukuro Station East Exit

¥¥ Lunch ¥1,000–¥2,000

No bookings and cash only — and it closes early when it sells out, so we go early if this is the pick.

Sushi · conveyor belt

Kura Sushi

無添くら寿司 池袋サンシャイン60通り店

Conveyor-belt sushi, the big-chain way: Kura Sushi is one of Japan’s giant kaiten chains, on the 6th floor of the Santropez Ikebukuro building on Sunshine 60-dori, about five minutes on foot from Sunshine City. You order plate by plate on the touch panel — plates from ¥145 — so everyone eats exactly as much as they want. And every five empty plates earns a spin of Bikkura Pon, the capsule game at the table — the 11-year-old will have a plan for lunch. Booth seats, and a lift in the building for the way up.

11:00 to 24:00, every day (from 10:20 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays)
Higashi-Ikebukuro 1-29-1, Santropez Ikebukuro 6F — about 5 min from Sunshine City

¥¥ Around ¥1,500–¥2,000 a person if you eat your fill — and less if you don’t

This one takes bookings — want it? Let me know in advance and I’ll book us in.
